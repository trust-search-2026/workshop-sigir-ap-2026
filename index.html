Overview

Search agents do more than retrieve documents: they organize evidence, form intermediate conclusions, and use those conclusions to decide what to search next. An early error can therefore bias later retrieval and become self-reinforcing.

TRUST-Search 2026 studies how to make this search trajectory reliable. Its central theme is a loop in which retrieved evidence supports reasoning and pattern discovery, while further search or external tools challenge the resulting claims. Verification is treated not as a final fact-check, but as a source of new queries and revised reasoning.

Search→ Evidence→ Reasoning / Discovery→ Verification→ Revised Search ↺

The key step is the return to search. A system should ask what could contradict its current conclusion, which independent source could test it, and what evidence is still missing. A failed check should lead to a revised claim, a new query, or an explicit statement that the evidence is insufficient.

Why it matters for IR

The theme concerns query formation, source selection, evidence coverage, provenance, counter-evidence retrieval, stopping rules, and the evaluation of multi-step search trajectories. The workshop is not a general forum on trustworthy AI: its focus is how evidence is acquired, interpreted, challenged, and revisited during autonomous information seeking.

Special focus

Neuro-symbolic methods that combine neural search and hypothesis generation with symbolic, formal, or executable checks — with AI for Mathematics and AI for Science as demanding test cases, where a new claim often has no reference answer.

Topics of Interest

We welcome work on any part of the search–discovery–verification loop, including but not limited to:

01

Retrieval & RAG as the Foundation

Source dependence, evidence coverage, conflicting information, attribution, ranking for independent or counter-evidence, and recognizing when a collection cannot justify a reliable answer.

02

Agentic Search & Organized Reasoning

Task decomposition, multi-query search, source comparison, tool use, inspectable intermediate findings, and revisiting earlier branches instead of locking in weak assumptions.

03

Pattern & Anomaly Discovery

Treating candidate patterns as tentative objects to investigate; distinguishing observations, inferred relations, hypotheses, and conclusions that survive a stated check.

04

Neuro-Symbolic Verification

Knowledge-graph rules, computer algebra, programs, constraint solvers, proof assistants, and scientific models as checkers — and how their feedback should change the search trajectory.

05

Trajectory Evaluation

Evaluating beyond the final answer: are intermediate claims supported, was counter-evidence sought, was formal feedback used correctly, and did a failed check cause meaningful revision?

06

AI for Mathematics & Science

Evidence lineage from source to claim, genuinely independent evidence, labeling outputs as observation, hypothesis, or verified result, and evaluating new claims without reference answers.

Related Workshops

TRUST-Search complements IR-RAG and R3AG (retrieval and reliability in RAG), BREV-RAG (evaluation beyond relevance), AgentIR (agent-based IR), Search Futures, and AgentSearch. Its distinctive unit of analysis is the search trajectory: evidence is retrieved, interpreted, turned into a candidate claim, challenged by further search or an external check, and revised when that check fails.
